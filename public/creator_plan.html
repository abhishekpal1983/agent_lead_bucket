How the conversion benchmark works
A conversion rate measured per stage is not recoverable from this CRM. Three routes were tried; all are circular or unavailable:
  • previous_engagement_stage is self-referencing on 325 of 339 wins.
  • Matching paying consumers back to HubSpot puts 253 of 256 matches in deal_won — the stage is overwritten the moment someone pays, destroying the pre-payment stage.
  • True stage history needs propertiesWithHistory batch reads, which this connector does not expose.
So each creator is anchored on its own measured lead-to-enrolment rate, split national and international. Tier defaults are the ayush tier shape rescaled to that creator's measured level, so a creator converting at a third of ayush's rate starts with tier assumptions a third as high.

Each tier shows its multiple of the matching baseline. Late-funnel tiers deserve to run high — a payment prospect genuinely converts far better than an average lead — but a high multiple marks a claim you are asserting, not one the data proves.

To make this measurable, write a stage_at_payment field when a payment lands.
Data quality and naming
  • Two handles in the original brief do not exist in HubSpot. kartikapoorconsultation is really kartikkapoorconsultation (1,136 leads) and simrankhoka is really simrankhokha (159 leads).
  • 19% of ayush revenue is unattributed — 60 of 316 paying consumers have no HubSpot contact under that creator.
  • deal_won overstates enrolment by about 25%: 339 ayush contacts marked won against 253 with a payment behind them. The model is anchored on the payment sheet for this reason.
  • Thin international samples. Where a creator has under 200 international leads or under 5 international enrolments, the measured international rate is unreliable and is set to 0.48x national. Those creators are flagged above.
  • Sheet composition. 317 of 1,503 payment rows are loan, ongoing, manual or upcoming rather than completed, and all are counted; 48 rows carry price_inr of 1.
